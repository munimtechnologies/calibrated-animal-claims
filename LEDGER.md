# Ledger: calibrated re-tests of headline animal-communication claims

MV2, Munim, Inc. (mv2@munimtech.com). Compiled on 2026-11-22, updated 2026-11-28 (version 16), with AI agents working in MV2's name; not yet checked by a human expert. Text and data CC BY 4.0. The machine-readable version, with the exact source file and key of every number, is [results/ledger.json](results/ledger.json); `python -m unittest -v test_ledger` checks it.

**What this is.** Each row is a published claim that one of MV2's open projects re-tested with a pre-registered, calibrated test on the authors' own public data. "Published-style procedure" is the published test, or our closest reconstruction of it. "False-alarm rate" is how often that procedure reported the effect in worlds with the effect removed but the suspected structure (bouts, days, callers, social groups, recording sessions, rhythm mixtures, unequal button use, pair repertoires, females as units, few clans, each bird's finite-memory syntax, pooled birds, heavy-tailed bouts, the measurement window, slow state shared by songs sung close in time) kept. Most worlds are built from the real data; the birdsong rows also use registered simulated worlds. Claims that an effect is **absent** are listed separately, with the power of the calibrated test.

**Selection warning.** We picked these claims because we suspected their tests. The rates say nothing about the field as a whole, and each world embodies one specific confound.

## Negative claims: calibrated test and its power

| claim | calibrated test (registered) | planted effect found | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyena whoops carry no clan signature (Lehmann et al. 2022) | leave-one-hyena-out clan accuracy 0.2403 against a relabeling null of 0.2207, p 0.34; handheld only p 0.1767; the authors' scheme reproduced, excess p 0.09 | 40 of 40 worlds with a clan offset half the size of individual differences (hyena-clan-signature-test: results/hyena_results.json) | SURVIVES |

The paper reported this negative without a significance test or power analysis. It holds: no clan signal under a calibrated test that would have found a whole-whoop clan offset even a quarter the size of individual differences (40 of 40 worlds at every planted size).

## Reading

Unsafe tests were the rule in this set, but most of the effects they reported were real anyway. Of the fifteen claims a calibrated test could decide, eleven held in full or in part, including the one negative claim (hyena clans). Four failed. The chimpanzee additive pairs failed because their criterion cannot tell "A and B at once" from "sometimes A, sometimes B". The magpie second-order claim failed at the level of sequence types, and both of the preprint's own procedures, rebuilt from its Methods, would have reported it anyway. The marmoset second-order claim is the mirror image: its cross-validated test never fired in 1,996 worlds without second-order rules. The soundboard dogs' network evidence failed because the comparison graphs give every button the same chance of use. The Bengalese finch hierarchy evidence (version 10) failed because each bird's own finite-memory syntax (a memory of 2 to 8 syllables), with bouts joined by day as in the paper, already produces the exponential-plus-power-law curve; hierarchy is not ruled out, but this statistic cannot see it there. Versions 12 and 14 show why in general: in registered simulations the verdict fires on pooled finite-state birds, persistent bouts and heavy-tailed bouts, and mostly misses standard recursive models, whose curve is usually a pure power law instead. Stage 8 tested that pure power law as a replacement on 650 fresh worlds: it fired in 133 of 250 recursive worlds, never on heavy-tailed bouts, and in 5 of 350 finite-state worlds, but one finite-state design (pooled birds, 3 of 50) went past the registered limit of 0.05, so the registered grade is MIXED and it is not yet a calibrated signature. Version 15 adds the positive control that was missing: human speech, hierarchical by any account, does not give the verdict at bird-data size (about 190,000 phones; a plain exponential won every time). The 2019 speech result pooled 4,379,552 conversational phones; how much speech the verdict needs is registered as stage 6B.

The parrot duets and the magpie group repertoires show a third case: **the effect is real but the published evidence does not measure it.** In the parrot duets that order also survives every registered robustness check (version 11). The finch repeat-number links (version 16) may be another: the preprint's control reports links in 0.875 of slow-state-only worlds, yet 92 of 110 links survive a null that keeps the state of neighbouring songs. Our null is not calibrated, so that row stays undecided. The dolphin result, the delta-band tempo and the conserved call rhythm are the plain case: we expected the effect to rest on one female or on the measurable window, and it does not. For the call rhythm (version 13) the published model choice is weak on its own (it fires in 36.5% of window-only worlds), yet the real clustering of rhythms near 3 Hz is far tighter than the window allows. Version 9 added a fourth kind: **a correctly sized test of the wrong comparison.** The sperm whale clan Mantel test is exact and reproduces to four decimals, and the identity-coda trend survives a sampling-effort control, but symbolic marking needs identity codas to respond to overlap more than other codas, and that difference (p 0.091 on 7 clans) was never tested. A within-clan test we added first seemed to support marking and turned out to be circular. Five claims stay undecided: three because our own registered test failed its calibration (the sperm whale coarticulation amendment is now calibrated but underpowered; the finch links null leaks under strong slow state), and two because the public data are too thin (elephant names, sperm whale clans).

## Prior art

Pooling and pseudoreplication in animal-communication statistics are well documented: Mundry and Sommer 2007 (Animal Behaviour, doi:10.1016/j.anbehav.2006.12.028), Waller et al. 2013 (Animal Behaviour, doi:10.1016/j.anbehav.2013.05.038), Whitehouse et al. 2023 (International Journal of Primatology, doi:10.1007/s10764-023-00399-y), Jadoul et al. 2025 on the uniform null behind integer-ratio rhythm analyses (Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences, doi:10.1111/nyas.70037), and for one headline claim, Wartel et al. 2026 on bonobo compositionality (PeerJ, doi:10.7717/peerj.21651). That clustering falling with degree can come from degree structure rather than hierarchy is Soffer and Vazquez 2005 (Physical Review E 71:057101). A defined order of sex-specific notes in yellow-naped amazon duets was described by Wright and Dahlin 2007 (Behaviour 144:207-228, doi:10.1163/156853907779947346). That Markov processes give exponentially decaying mutual information, and the recursive model we use as a positive control, are from Lin and Tegmark 2017 (Entropy 19:299). That power-law correlations need not mean hierarchy in human language is argued by Nakaishi et al. (arXiv 2505.04984); Morita et al. 2021 (PLoS Computational Biology 17:e1009707) measured a Bengalese finch song context of about eight syllables. Howard-Spink et al. 2024 ([PeerJ 12:e18484](https://peerj.com/articles/18484/)) first measured how often a single Markov chain gives the power-law verdict, on chimpanzee actions; the birdsong calibration extends that check to birdsong designs, a recursive control and human speech. That Brownian motion between bounds can be mistaken for Ornstein-Uhlenbeck selection is Boucher and Demery 2016 (Systematic Biology 65:651); the window model of the rhythm row is theirs. Repeat-number models of Bengalese finch song are Jin and Kozhevnikov 2011 and Wittenbach et al. 2015 (PLoS Computational Biology). What this ledger adds is a measured false-alarm rate, claim by claim, for recent headline results, next to a calibrated verdict on the effect itself.
